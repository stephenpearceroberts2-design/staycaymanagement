What does a holiday let management company actually do?

14 October 20266 min readBy the Staycay team

If you own a holiday home, you'll know that the work doesn't stop when the booking comes in. Someone has to answer the questions, arrange the changeover, check the property, fix the boiler on a Saturday and decide what to charge for the half-term week. A holiday let management company takes on some or all of that work for you.

Here's what that usually covers, and how to decide which level of help suits you and your home.

Guests, from enquiry to review

The most visible part of the job is looking after guests: answering enquiries, confirming bookings, sending arrival information, being on hand during the stay and following up afterwards. Good guest communication is quick, warm and specific to your home. It's also where many owners feel the strain first, because guests expect replies at any hour.

Housekeeping and changeovers

Every stay ends with a changeover: cleaning, fresh linen, restocking and a final check. In a busy season that can mean several turnarounds a week, often on tight timings. A manager organises and checks this work so the home is ready, and consistent, for every arrival.

Property care and maintenance

Holiday homes work hard. Regular inspections catch small problems before they turn into bad reviews, and a manager with a network of trusted local trades can get things fixed quickly. You stay in control of significant decisions and spend.

Pricing, revenue and distribution

This is where the difference between a good year and a great one is often made. Rates should move with demand: school holidays, local events, weekends, short-notice gaps. Listings need to be in the right places, with minimum stays and availability set to reduce empty nights. Revenue management is a specialist skill, and some owners use a manager for this alone.

Presentation and marketing

Guests choose with their eyes. Professional photography, a well-written description and a home that is dressed with care all affect how often you're booked and what you can charge.

Full management or bookings-only?

Broadly, there are two models. Full management covers everything above, so the home earns without you running it day to day. Bookings and revenue management suits owners who are happy to look after the property themselves but want expert help with pricing, listings and bookings.

We've written more about choosing between the two.

What to look for in a manager

  • Local knowledge. Someone who knows the area, its seasons and its guests will price and present your home better.
  • Standards you can see. Ask how they inspect properties and what happens when something goes wrong.
  • Clear reporting. You should always know how your home is performing.
  • Selectiveness. A manager who takes on every property can't give each one the same attention.
